    How the colours and the score work

    Each hazard becomes a number from 0 (no concern) to 1 (severe):

      Own colour: the stronger the hazard, the stronger its colour. Where hazards overlap, the colours mix. Combined score: the weighted average of the selected hazards, times 100.

      The thresholds and weights are my choice, not a scientific standard. Use them to find places to look at, then check each value.

      Data sources
      • Summer heat, warm nights, yearly rainfall (1 km): CHELSA v2.1 climate for 1981-2010 at 30 arc-seconds. For 2050, I add the change from the World Bank CMIP6 data (median of about 25 models, 2040-2059 minus 1995-2014), smoothly interpolated. This is the standard "delta" method.
      • Very hot days, tropical nights, drought, dry spells, heavy rain (25 km): World Bank Climate Change Knowledge Portal, CMIP6, bias-corrected and downscaled (NASA NEX-GDDP), 0.25° grid.
      • Water stress (per river basin): WRI Aqueduct 4.0. Today = baseline. Moderate map = "business as usual" (SSP3-7.0), high map = "pessimistic" (SSP5-8.5). Aqueduct has no SSP2-4.5 run.
      • River flood zone (1 km) and depth (90 m): Copernicus EMS / JRC river flood hazard maps for Europe v3.1, 100-year flood, present-day climate. Lakes removed.
      • River flood trend (5 km): JRC PESETA IV (Mentaschi et al. 2020). Change of the 100-year river flow at 2 °C (moderate) and 2.4 °C (high) warming. Large rivers only (50 m³/s or more).
      • Coastal flood zone (1 km) and depth (450 m): my calculation. Land below the local 100-year extreme sea level (JRC LISCoAsT, median, includes sea level rise, tide, surge and waves) that connects to the sea. Elevation: NOAA ETOPO 2022.
      • Forest loss: Hansen / University of Maryland via Global Forest Watch. Burnt areas: EFFIS. Both are live services.
      • Cities: Natural Earth. Base map: Esri World Gray Canvas.

      Each source keeps its own licence and asks for credit (for example WRI Aqueduct: CC BY 4.0, JRC flood maps: ODbL 1.0). If you reuse this map, keep these credits. Code and build steps: GitHub.

      Limits - read before you decide
      • Only the layers marked "1 km" or finer have local detail. The 25 km layers average coast and inland, valley and mountain.
      • The 1 km "today" climate is 1981-2010. The 2050 change is measured from 1995-2014, so 2050 heat values are probably about 0.3 °C too low.
      • Flood maps ignore dikes and flood walls. The Netherlands shows as mostly flooded, but strong defences protect it. The coastal model is simple and can overstate flooding in flat areas.
      • Climate values are the median of many models. Real outcomes can be higher or lower.
      • Not included: future wildfire danger, storms, landslides, city heat islands, air quality, insurance cost, economy and politics.
